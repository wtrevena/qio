## 1. Experiment 1 — Matching manifold characterization (N = 10⁷ Haar states)

- 1,666,906 states had the required ordering S₁ > S₂ > S₃ (≈ 1/6, as expected).
- 2,666 states matched |r_S − 1.8174| < 0.01 (match rate 2.7 × 10⁻⁴;
  1.6 × 10⁻³ given ordering). Matching is easy, exactly as the
  Underconstraint Lemma predicts.
- **The matching manifold is statistically generic.** The 3-tangle
  distribution of matched states is nearly identical to ordering-matched
  Haar controls: KS statistic 0.031 (p = 0.023, a negligible effect size at
  these sample sizes); means 0.345 vs 0.334; medians 0.326 vs 0.312.
  The fraction with τ₃ < 0.01 is ~0.1% in both populations.
- Pairwise concurrences shift as forced by the entropy-gap conditioning
  (C₁₂, C₁₃ up; C₂₃ slightly up), with no signature of any special locus.

**Conclusion: Requirement C (non-genericity) of Sec 5.4 fails for
unconstrained matching.** This converts the paper's analytic underconstraint
argument into a measured fact.

The octonion multiplication table was generated by Cayley–Dickson doubling
(machine-verified: e_a e_b = ±e_{a⊕b}), so the structure constants are
convention-independent up to basis relabeling.

**Two sharp results:**

1. **Every canonical octonionic construction tested yields
   permutation-degenerate entropies** (all equal, or {0,0,0}/{1,1,0}/{1,1,1}
   patterns). None can produce a three-way hierarchy; r_S is undefined (0/0)
   on all of them. The algebra's symmetry (G₂ automorphisms, triality,
   XOR-grading of the basis) is too rigid: hierarchy requires symmetry
   breaking beyond choosing a preferred unit. This sharpens Requirement A —
   the needed selection principle cannot be any of the obvious
   "canonical state" maps.

2. **The matching manifold intersects the W-class (τ₃ = 0) sector**: the
   weighted-W family contains a continuous one-parameter curve of states with
   r_S = 1.8174 exactly (e.g. S = (0.978, 0.895, 0.849), weights
   (a², b², c²) ≈ given in `results/exp2_results.json`). So coupling matching
   does not even discriminate between SLOCC classes — both GHZ-class and
   W-class realizations exist.

## 3. Null controls

- **A (random targets, 300 log-uniform coupling triples):** SM-target matched
  states have mean τ₃ = 0.320 vs 0.337 ± 0.018 across random targets;
  z = −0.92. The SM hierarchy is statistically indistinguishable from random
  coupling targets.
- **B (two-qubit):** any two-qubit *pure* state has S₁ = S₂ identically
  (Schmidt decomposition; numerically max |S₁−S₂| = 4 × 10⁻¹⁵ over 10⁵
  states). The two-coupling logarithmic map with B ≠ 0 is therefore
  *impossible*, not merely underconstrained. **New analytic point for the
  paper: three qubits is the minimal system on which the ansatz has any
  content.**
- **C (four-qubit):** per-triple match rate 3.4 × 10⁻⁴ vs 2.6 × 10⁻⁴ for
  three qubits; allowing any of the 4 ordered triples, 1.3 × 10⁻³. Four-qubit
  systems match at least as easily — parameter freedom, not
  division-algebra structure, drives matching.
- **D (label permutations):** all 6 qubit-gauge assignments match at
  statistically identical rates (256–292 per 10⁶), as exchange symmetry of
  the Haar measure requires. The Szangolies labeling currently carries no
  measurable content for unconstrained states.

## 4. Net effect on the paper

Every experiment came out on the "negative/diagnostic" side, which is the
side the paper already argues. The program can now be reported as *completed*
with quantitative results, upgrading Section 6 from proposal to evidence, and
adding two new analytic observations (two-qubit impossibility; permutation
rigidity of canonical octonionic states).
