# Flux-sector statistics of compact U(1): exponential-family structure and Fisher information
## Structural layer (analytic), supporting the reviewer's question for d = 2+1 and d = 3+1

**Conventions.** Kogut–Susskind Hamiltonian for compact U(1) on a spatial cubic lattice (a = 1),
links l, plaquettes p:

    H = (e²/2) Σ_l E_l²  −  (1/(2e²)) Σ_p (U_p + U_p†),      E_l|n_l⟩ = n_l|n_l⟩, n_l ∈ ℤ,

U_p = product of e^{iθ_l} around p; U_p raises the four (d=3+1) or four (d=2+1) link fluxes of p
by ±1 around the loop. Extended-Hilbert-space cut: region A = set of links; the center of the
gauge-invariant algebra of A is generated by {E_l : l crossing the entangling surface Σ}; ρ_A is
block-diagonal in the cut fluxes {n_l}_{l∈∂} (Gauss-constrained: total flux out of A through a
*closed* Σ equals the charge inside = 0). The object of study is the induced distribution
p_{e²}({n_l}) and its statistical geometry in the coupling.

**Definition (used throughout).** A one-parameter family p_η(x) is an *exponential family* iff it
can be written p_η(x) = h(x) exp[−θ(η) T(x)] / Z(η) with η-independent h and T. This is a
property of the family of distributions, invariant under smooth reparametrizations η → η′; "the
natural parameter" θ is fixed up to affine maps. So "exponential family in 1/e²" and "exponential
family in e²" are the same property; what is *not* automatic is which function of e² the natural
parameter is, and that function is what the Fisher information tracks.

**Lemma (entropy–Fisher identity, generalizing 2d YM).** For p_θ(x) = h(x)e^{−θT(x)}/Z(θ) with
natural parameter θ:

    I(θ) = Var_θ(T),     dS/dθ = −θ·Var_θ(T) − Cov_θ(ln h, T).

If h ≡ 1 on the support, **dS/dθ = −θ·I(θ)** — identical in form to the 2d YM identity
dS/dt = −t·I(t) (where θ = t/2 up to scale, T = C₂, h = d_R² = 1 for U(1)). Proof: three lines of
cumulant algebra, as in ym2/NOTE.md §2; nothing about 2d enters. The 2d-specific content of
Paper 3 was never the identity but (i) that the *exact ground state* is an exponential family at
all couplings, and (ii) that t is the natural parameter.

## A. Warm-up / control: d = 1+1 (the exact case) and what must generalize

2d YM (U(1)): p_n(t) ∝ e^{−(t/2)n²}, t = e²A. Exponential family at *every* coupling, natural
parameter linear in e², sufficient statistic the Casimir n², single global flux variable (no area
scaling of information). Everything below measures which of these four features survives.

---

## B. d = 3+1 strong-coupling (electric) limit: exact leading-order structure

### B.1 Ground state

At e² → ∞ the electric term dominates; the unperturbed ground state is |0⟩ (all n_l = 0).
Plaquette excitation |p,±⟩ (flux ±1 around the four links of p) costs ΔE = (e²/2)·4 = 2e².
First-order perturbation theory in V = −(1/(2e²))Σ_p(U_p + U_p†):

    |Ω⟩ ∝ |0⟩ + c Σ_{p,σ=±} |p,σ⟩ + O(c²),     c = (1/(2e²)) / (2e²) = 1/(4e⁴).

Higher orders exponentiate cluster-wise: to leading order in each connected cluster,
ψ({n}) ∝ c^{k({n})} where k({n}) is the minimal number of plaquette applications producing the
configuration (the standard strong-coupling counting; corrections multiply each amplitude by
1 + O(e⁻⁴)).

### B.2 Flux-sector distribution across a flat cut

Let Σ be a plane crossing N_∂ z-links. Each plaquette "straddling" Σ (xz- or yz-oriented,
spanning the cut; there are N_str = 2N_∂ of them) contains exactly **two** cut links, on which an
excitation deposits the dipole (+1, −1). Therefore, at leading order:

- single dipole on an adjacent pair via plaquette p, orientation σ: probability ∝ c²;
- stacked/multiple dipoles: probability ∝ c^{2k};
- a config {n_l} requires k({n}) = ½ Σ_l |n_l| plaquette excitations (each dipole contributes
  |+1| + |−1| = 2), whenever {n} is dipole-reachable; non-reachable configs are higher order.

So the leading-order flux distribution is

    **p({n_l}) = (1/Z) · h({n}) · exp[ −θ(e²) · T({n}) ],
       T({n}) = ½ Σ_{l∈∂} |n_l|,   θ(e²) = −ln c² = 2 ln(4e⁴) = ln 16 + 8 ln e,**

with h({n}) ∈ {0,1} the dipole-reachability indicator at this order (h = 1 on the support, so the
Lemma applies with no Cov term). Three structural conclusions:

1. **Exponential family: YES at leading order** — but the sufficient statistic is the *ℓ¹ flux*
   ½Σ|n_l| (dipole count), **not** the quadratic Casimir Σn_l², and the natural parameter is
   **θ = 8 ln e + ln 16, logarithmic in the coupling — not e², and not 1/e²**. The naive
   transplant of the 2d YM form p ∝ e^{−(e²/2)ΣC₂} is *wrong* in the 4d electric limit.
2. **Identity: dS/dθ = −θ·I(θ) holds exactly at this order** (h ≡ 1 on support), with
   I(θ) = Var(T). In terms of e² it reads dS/de² = −θ(e²)·(8/e²)... i.e. the clean form lives
   only in the natural parameter; "dS/d(e²) = −e²·I(e²)" is **false** here.
3. **Information is extensive in the boundary area** (the decisive difference from d = 2):
   straddling plaquettes are independent at leading order, each a 3-state system
   {0, +dipole, −dipole} with weights (1−2q, q, q)/1, q = c²/(1+2c²)·(1+O(c²)). Hence
       I(θ) = Var(T) = N_str · 2q(1−2q) ≈ 2N_str c² = N_str/(8e⁸),
       S_class = N_str·[−(1−2q)ln(1−2q) − 2q ln q] ≈ 2N_str c²(1 + ln(1/c²)).
   Per unit physical area, I ∝ a⁻² × (e-dependent factor): each lattice cell of the surface is an
   independent sample. The 2d pathology "entropy scales, information does not" is gone; in d = 4
   both scale with the area.

Translation dictionary: information about e² itself is I(e²) = (dθ/de²)² I(θ) = (4/e²)²·2N_str c²
= 2N_str/e¹² — nonzero and finite at any fixed lattice spacing, exponentially small in ln e (power
law in 1/e), vanishing as e → ∞ (the sectors freeze to n ≡ 0: zero susceptibility, zero
information, S → 0 — the same t → ∞ corner as 2d YM).

### B.3 Where exponential-family structure fails

At next order in 1/e⁴: (i) amplitudes for stacked dipoles, L-shaped 1×2 loop excitations, and
plaquettes parallel to Σ at distance 1 acquire distinct O(1) prefactors → the base measure h({n})
becomes a nontrivial (still θ-independent at fixed order, but order-mixing) function and
cross-link correlations appear that are not functions of T alone; (ii) the energy denominators
make the "natural parameter per cluster type" differ at O(e⁻⁴): the family becomes **curved** —
log-likelihoods at different couplings are no longer confined to a 2-plane spanned by {1, T}.
This is quantified numerically in the 2+1d model below (the same mechanism). Honest summary:
exponential family is an *asymptotic* property of both ends of the coupling axis, with computable
curvature in between; nothing protects it at intermediate coupling (in 2d YM it was protected by
the self-reproducing heat kernel — there is no analog here).

## C. d = 3+1 weak coupling (Coulomb phase)

By Guth / Fröhlich–Spencer, for β = 1/e² > β_c the lattice theory is in a massless Coulomb phase:
free photon + dilute gapped monopoles. Two equivalent routes (compact QED ↔ ℤ-gauge-restricted
Gaussian) give the ground state in the electric representation as a **constrained discrete
Gaussian**: up to monopole corrections e^{−S_mono} (exponentially small in 1/e²),

    **p({n_l}) ≈ (1/Z) exp[ −(e²/2) Σ_{l,l'∈∂} n_l (M⁻¹)_{ll'} n_{l'} ]**, restricted to Σ n = 0
    (closed Σ, no charges),

where M is the boundary restriction of the photon ground-state covariance: ⟨E E⟩ = (1/e²)·M with
M built from the lattice kernel √(−Δ) — a *pure-geometry* object, e-independent because the
photon is free and the coupling does not run in the pure compact theory's Coulomb phase. (The
zero-mode/topological version of this statement is exactly Donnelly–Wall 1506.05792 eq. (36):
Z_E = Σ_{E∈q_Bℤ} e^{−½Vol(B)E²}, i.e. p(n) ∝ e^{−(q_B²Vol(B)/2)n²}, natural parameter ∝ q².)

Consequences:

1. **Exponential family: YES, asymptotically** — sufficient statistic the *quadratic form*
   T = ½ n·M⁻¹·n (the Casimir structure reappears at weak coupling, now with a nonlocal geometric
   kernel), natural parameter **θ = e² exactly** (not 1/e²; the reviewer's "exponential family in
   1/e²" is correct as a family statement, with the natural parameter being e² — and dually, the
   *magnetic* flux sectors have natural parameter ∝ 1/e², by electromagnetic duality and as in
   DW eq. (37)/(36)-magnetic).
2. **Identity: dS/de² = −e²·I(e²) holds asymptotically** with h ≡ 1 (the Gauss constraint is an
   e-independent restriction of the support, harmless). This is the cleanest 4d descendant of
   dS/dt = −t·I(t): same form, coupling-squared as natural parameter, with t = e²A replaced by
   θ = e² and the Casimir replaced by the M⁻¹-quadratic form.
3. **Fisher information: per boundary mode, Gaussian asymptotics give I(e²) ≈ 1/(2e⁴)**
   (log-likelihood −(e²/2)λ_i x² + ½ln e² per eigenmode of M⁻¹; valid when the mode variance
   λ_i⁻¹/e² ≫ 1, i.e. deep weak coupling). The number of independent boundary flux modes is
   N_∂ − 1 ∝ Area/a². Hence
       **I_total(e²) ≈ (Area/a²)·(1/(2e⁴))** → **divergent in the continuum limit a → 0** at
   fixed physical surface and fixed e.
4. **Why this does not contradict the free-field no-go:** the rescaling E → E/e that removes e
   from noncompact Maxwell is forbidden here because n_l ∈ ℤ — *flux quantization provides the
   absolute unit* in which the Gaussian's width is measured. All e-dependence of the flux
   statistics is a compactness effect, exactly as anticipated.
5. **Physical status (the CHMP/MST caveat):** the divergent information is carried by
   cutoff-scale flux fluctuations in the center/edge sector — precisely the non-distillable data
   that drops out of mutual information and relative entropy in the continuum limit
   (MST 1811.06986) and whose entropy assignment is center-choice dependent (CHR 1312.1183). The
   *universal* continuum quantities forget e (CHMP 1911.00529: the charge-induced correction to
   the log coefficient is coupling-independent). The IR remnant that survives as
   cutoff-independent data is the **topological/zero-mode family** of DW (one discrete-Gaussian
   label per homology class of Σ / per closed surface), which carries *finite* Fisher information
   I_zero-mode(e²) — the d = 4 quantity most literally analogous to Paper 3's p_R(t), and the one
   we propose as the well-defined target ("Fisher information per zero mode").

So in the Coulomb phase the answer is three-tiered: **lattice-regulated edge data: I ∝ Area/a²·
e⁻⁴ (divergent in the continuum); IR/topological zero modes: finite nonzero I; universal
continuum coefficients: zero I.**

## D. d = 2+1 (Polyakov): the tractable middle case and the numerical model

Dual (height) representation: on a 2d spatial lattice, solving Gauss's law sets E_l = h_p − h_p′
(difference of integer dual heights on the two faces adjacent to l; fixed boundary h ≡ 0). The KS
Hamiltonian becomes a quantum integer-height (discrete Gaussian / rotor) model

    H = (e²/2) Σ_{⟨pp′⟩} (h_p − h_p′)²  −  (1/(2e²)) Σ_p (R_p + R_p†),

with R_p: h_p → h_p + 1 (R_p = e^{iθ_p}, the dual of the plaquette operator). A strip of N
plaquettes (1×N ladder, the minimal 2+1d geometry) gives a chain h_1…h_N with h_0 = h_{N+1} = 0:
the model diagonalized in `u1_chain_ed.py`. The entangling cut between plaquettes k and k+1 cuts
exactly one bulk link, whose flux is the center label:

    n = h_k − h_{k+1} ∈ ℤ,     p_{e²}(n) = Σ_{h: h_k−h_{k+1}=n} |ψ(h)|².

Analytic anchors for the numerics:

- **Strong coupling:** single-site excitation costs e² (two links), amplitude
  c₃ = (1/(2e²))/e² = 1/(2e⁴). Two configurations give n = ±1 (h_k = ±1 or h_{k+1} = ∓1), so
      p(±1)/p(0) → 2c₃² = 1/(2e⁸),   d ln[p(1)/p(0)] / d ln e = −8,
  and p(n) ~ (const)·c₃^{2|n|}: sufficient statistic |n|, natural parameter θ ≈ 8 ln e + ln 2 +
  …, same logarithmic structure as d = 3+1 (B.2). The 3-state reduction {0,±1} of each boundary
  mode is the same dipole physics with the strip's single cut link.
- **Weak coupling:** the chain is a harmonic (discrete-Gaussian) chain; p(n) → discrete Gaussian
  with ⟨n²⟩ ∝ 1/e²; natural parameter ∝ e², sufficient statistic n² (with finite-size kernel
  corrections; in the infinite 2+1d system the Polyakov mass m(e²) ∝ e^{−const/e²} enters the
  kernel shape, making the family weakly *curved* — the kernel runs with e, unlike the 4d Coulomb
  phase where it is frozen. On a small chain this IR effect is replaced by finite-size gaps).
- **In between:** no protection; the curvature of the family is measured by the rank test below.

**Numerical tests implemented** (`u1_chain_ed.py`, `analyze_chain.py`):
1. *Rank/curvature test of exponential-family structure:* assemble L[j,n] = ln p(n; e²_j) over a
   coupling grid; the family is exponential iff the rows of D[j,n] = L[j,n] − L[j₀,n] lie in
   span{1(n), T(n)} for a fixed T, i.e. rank(D) = 2. Report σ₃/σ₂ of the SVD (exact exponential
   family: 0 up to numerics; 2d YM control: machine zero).
2. *Strong-coupling slope:* d ln[p(1)/p(0)]/d ln e² → −4 (i.e. −8 in ln e).
3. *Weak-coupling parameter:* linearity of ln[p(1)/p(0)] in e² (natural parameter ∝ e²).
4. *Fisher information* I(e²) by central differences of ln p; entropy H[p(n)]; test of
   dS/dθ = −θ·Var(T) with (θ_j, T) extracted from the rank-2 fit; report the residual as the
   measured violation (= curvature + base-measure covariance).
5. Convergence in the height cutoff h_max and in N; deterministic Lanczos (seed 20260610).

## E. Answers to the structural questions (to be cross-checked by the numerics)

**(Q) Is the ground-state flux distribution an exponential family in a function of e²?**
- d=1+1: yes, exactly, at all couplings (natural parameter e²A; Casimir statistic). [Paper 3]
- d=3+1 electric limit: yes at leading order in 1/e⁴ (natural parameter ln(16e⁸); ℓ¹ statistic
  ½Σ|n|; NOT the Casimir, NOT linear in e²); curved at higher orders.
- d=3+1 Coulomb phase: yes up to e^{−S_mono} corrections (natural parameter e²; quadratic
  statistic with frozen geometric kernel; Gauss-constrained discrete Gaussian); the magnetic dual
  sectors have natural parameter ∝ 1/e².
- d=2+1: yes asymptotically at both ends; curved in between and (in the thermodynamic limit)
  weakly curved even at weak coupling via the Polyakov mass in the kernel.
- **Generic verdict: "conditionally yes" — exponential-family structure is an asymptotic,
  phase-by-phase property with computable curvature, not an exact identity as in d = 2. The
  natural parameter is e² at weak coupling but ln e at strong coupling; no single function of e²
  serves globally (the sufficient statistic itself crosses over from ½Σ|n| to ½n·M⁻¹n).**

**(Q) How much Fisher information about e does the flux distribution carry?**
- Strong coupling (either d): finite per boundary plaquette, I(θ) ≈ 2c²·N_str, extensive in the
  cut area; identity dS/dθ = −θI(θ) exact at leading order.
- Coulomb phase lattice: I(e²) ≈ N_modes/(2e⁴), extensive, **UV-divergent (∝ a⁻²) in the
  continuum limit**; finite information survives only in IR/topological zero modes; universal
  continuum coefficients carry **zero** information (CHMP).
- In all cases the information lives in the contested center/edge sector (non-distillable,
  center-choice-dependent); the d = 2 caveats import unchanged and sharpen: in d = 4 the
  *amount* of information is regulator-dependent, so only zero-mode/topological Fisher
  information is a candidate for a physical statement.
