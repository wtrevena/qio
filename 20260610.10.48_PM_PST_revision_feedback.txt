## Bottom line

I would **not submit the three manuscripts as three independent journal articles in their current form**. The work is much stronger than the original speculative question because it repeatedly narrows scope, proves no-go statements, and distinguishes “reconstruction” from “determination.” But as journal submissions, the series still reads too much like an internally evolving research program rather than a set of self-contained papers with external field-level motivation.

My publication recommendation would be:

| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -----------------------------------------------------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Paper 1: Three-qubit entropy no-go + rotor vacuum**             |                 **Reject / major reconceptualization** | Keep as arXiv background or merge the clean no-go parts with Paper 2. The rotor result should probably be separated or made appendix-only.                         |
| **Paper 2: Gauge-invariant algebraic entropy in the minimal toy** |                                     **Major revision** | Potentially publishable as a concise finite-dimensional algebraic-entanglement no-go paper, but title/scope need softening.                                        |

If the goal is **journal publication**, I would turn the series into **two papers**, not three:

1. **No-go paper:** combine the strongest parts of Papers 1 and 2 into one manuscript: “Frame dependence of three-qubit entropy matching and its gauge-invariant algebraic replacement.”
2. **Positive benchmark paper:** keep Paper 3 standalone: “Fisher information and coupling reconstruction from 2d Yang-Mills edge-sector statistics.”

The third manuscript, Paper 3, is the one I would lead with.

## Series-level assessment

The series has a coherent intellectual arc. Paper 1 kills the naive subsystem-entropy matching. Paper 2 repairs the question by moving to gauge-invariant algebraic entropy and then kills the repaired minimal toy. Paper 3 finds the one clean positive case, 2d Yang-Mills, where the edge/center flux distribution is an exact one-parameter family from which the coupling parameter can be reconstructed. The manuscripts themselves explicitly frame the sequence this way: Paper 1 says the subsystem-entropy question is ill-posed, Paper 2 says the well-posed algebraic version in the minimal toy has only charge-sector center data and no bulk quantum piece, and Paper 3 says the only surviving positive case is edge/flux statistics in exactly solvable 2d Yang-Mills.   

The strongest feature of the project is **intellectual honesty**. The manuscripts repeatedly deflate their own claims. Paper 1 says the object is not the Standard Model and that the three-qubit state is only a minimal-state ansatz. Paper 2 says the result is an eight-dimensional toy, not continuum gauge theory. Paper 3 states that its positive result is reconstruction inside a presupposed theory, not determination of the coupling by entanglement.   

The main weakness is that **the external target is still too narrow and internally generated**. Paper 1 critiques a proposed three-qubit entropy map motivated by Szangolies’s recent arXiv preprint on Standard Model symmetry and qubit entanglement; that preprint exists and does claim a three-qubit/octonionic construction with residual (SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)/\mathbb{Z}_6) symmetry, but it is a very recent preprint, not an established program. ([arXiv][1]) A journal referee may ask: “Why does this specific entropy-map ansatz deserve three papers?” The answer needs to be made much sharper.

## Paper 1 review: “Is the Standard Model Coupling Hierarchy Encoded in Three-Qubit Entanglement?”

### Summary

Paper 1 asks whether the measured gauge-coupling hierarchy can be encoded in the single-qubit entropies of a three-qubit candidate state via a two-parameter logarithmic map. It proves that the map collapses to a single entropy-gap-ratio condition, shows the target lies inside the feasible three-qubit marginal region, demonstrates genericity numerically, constructs an exact W-class family satisfying the ratio, argues that a scale-independent entropy triple is incompatible with RG running, and concludes that the real obstruction is gauge covariance: fermionic gauge transformations are not local unitaries of the Jordan-Wigner qubit tensor factors. It also includes a separate exact result on an octonionic rotor Hamiltonian with symmetric ground-state entanglement. 

### Strengths

The **underconstraint result is clean and correct in spirit**. Once two parameters (A,B) are fitted to three coupling values, the third condition is just one scalar ratio. That makes the “matching” non-evidential unless the state is independently selected.

The **W-class counterexample is a good diagnostic**. If a zero-3-tangle W-family can match the same entropy-gap ratio, then the matching cannot be selecting an octonionically distinguished entanglement class. This is exactly the kind of counterexample a referee would want.

The **gauge-covariance obstruction is the real result**. The paper’s strongest point is that single-qubit entropies are not gauge-invariant because the gauge action rotates fermionic modes rather than acting locally on the chosen qubit tensor factors. The manuscript should be reorganized around that fact.

The **scope language is unusually careful**. The paper explicitly says it is not deriving the Standard Model, not studying a true QFT vacuum, and not ruling out gauge-invariant or algebra-relative formulations. That helps credibility. 

### Major weaknesses

**1. The rotor vacuum result feels stapled on.**
The rotor Hamiltonian result may be mathematically interesting, but it is not essential to the no-go argument. The paper itself calls it “separate.” In journal form, that is a problem. A referee may read it as padding or as an unrelated exact observation inserted to add a positive result. I would either move it to an appendix, submit it as a separate short note, or make it much more explicitly a control showing that even the most canonical dynamical octonionic construction produces symmetry rather than hierarchy.

**2. The statistical Haar experiments are overkill and weaker than the analytic argument.**
The (10^7)-state genericity experiment is impressive computationally, but the core fact is geometric: a one-scalar condition inside a feasible entropy region defines a positive-dimensional matching set. The numerical genericity experiments should be reduced to a supporting sanity check, not presented as a central discovery.

**3. The target ansatz is too easy to kill.**
A two-parameter affine/log map from three single-qubit entropies to three couplings is so flexible and so ungrounded that disproving its evidential value may not be publishable unless tied tightly to an existing claim in the literature. The manuscript should explicitly state: “We are not refuting Szangolies’s construction; we are refuting a natural but naive coupling-entropy extension of it.”

**4. RG-running section risks looking obvious.**
The statement that a fixed entropy triple cannot match running couplings at all scales is true but not deep. The RG section should be shortened unless the affine-flow envelope produces a genuinely nontrivial theorem. Right now it reads like a long confirmation that the original map is not a physical model.

**5. The title overpromises.**
Even with the question mark, “Standard Model Coupling Hierarchy” is too broad for a paper about a toy entropy map. A safer title would be:

> “No-go results for a three-qubit entropy ansatz for gauge-coupling hierarchies”

or

> “Frame dependence and underconstraint in three-qubit entropy matching of gauge couplings”

### Publication verdict for Paper 1

I would **not submit Paper 1 as a standalone journal article yet**. It is valuable as an arXiv diagnostic paper and as background for Paper 2. For peer review, I would merge the essential pieces with Paper 2:

* underconstraint lemma,
* W-class counterexample,
* gauge-frame dependence,
* and maybe a short appendix on the rotor vacuum.

The current version is too sprawling for a journal article.

## Paper 2 review: “Gauge-Invariant Algebraic Entropy in a Minimal Fermionic Toy”

### Summary

Paper 2 repairs the main defect of Paper 1 by replacing subsystem entropies with entropy relative to the gauge-invariant observable algebra, i.e. the commutant of the gauge action. It computes the commutants for U(3), SU(3), and U(1) in the eight-dimensional Jordan-Wigner/Fock toy. It finds that U(3) leaves only charge-sector projectors, SU(3) leaves an (M_2\oplus\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}) algebra, and U(1) leaves (M_1\oplus M_3\oplus M_3\oplus M_1). It then shows that for pure states the quantum piece of the algebraic entropy vanishes for all three algebras, so the entropy is entirely a classical center/sector term. It also shows that the weighted-W matching curve from Paper 1 is pure gauge, and that no SU(2)(_L) action is present in the one-ideal, number-conserving three-mode realization. 

### Strengths

This is the **cleanest no-go paper** in the series. The finite-dimensional algebraic setup is concrete, the commutant computations are transparent, and the result “all edge, no bulk” is a crisp bridge to the gauge-theory entanglement literature.

The **commutant table is genuinely useful**. It turns the vague statement “make entanglement gauge-invariant” into a precise computation. The U(3), SU(3), and U(1) cases have clear interpretations: full mode rotations kill everything except charge-sector probabilities; SU(3) leaves the neutrino/positron singlet coherence; U(1) preserves within-sector color data. 

The **weighted-W collapse is the strongest link back to Paper 1**. Showing that the surviving entropy-matching curve is gauge-equivalent to a single Fock basis state directly explains why Paper 1’s apparent match was a frame artifact. 

The **SU(2)(_L) obstruction is important**. If the one-ideal toy only carries (U(3)\simeq(SU(3)*c\times U(1)*{em})/\mathbb{Z}_3), then a three-coupling question cannot be posed inside that toy. That is a strong scoped conclusion. 

### Major weaknesses

**1. The title says “closed,” but the paper itself says the multi-copy invariant ring remains open.**
The abstract and introduction are careful that the no-go closes the entropy version and the single-copy invariant-data version, not the full invariant-data version. But the title phrase “Made Well-Posed — and Closed” overstates. I would retitle it:

> “Gauge-invariant algebraic entropy in a minimal fermionic toy: a single-copy no-go for coupling encoding”

or

> “Algebra-relative entropy removes a three-qubit coupling-entropy artifact”

**2. The result may be considered too toy-specific.**
The manuscript says it is an eight-dimensional toy and claims nothing beyond it. That honesty is good, but it reduces journal impact. To make it publishable, the paper needs a broader lesson: not “this toy fails,” but “here is a general method for diagnosing whether an entanglement functional is gauge-invariant, applied to a prominent three-qubit construction.”

**3. “All edge, no bulk” may be mathematically almost immediate from the block structure.**
The theorem is correct, but once the Wedderburn blocks are known, the vanishing of the quantum piece for pure states follows quickly. The manuscript should avoid overselling this as a deep surprise. Its value is in the interpretation and in the connection to Paper 1, not in the entropy calculation alone.

**4. There is a public-repo consistency problem.**
The manuscript frames the two-ideal/chiral extension as future work or in progress, but the public repo already contains a `newwork/two_ideal/REPORT.md` file stating that the two-ideal extension is computed, machine-verified, and that it lifts the SU(2)(_L) obstruction. It also reports a 15-mode one-generation Standard Model Fock space, 250 sectors, nonzero quantum gauge-invariant entropy, and rank-3 variation of gauge-factor Casimir features. ([GitHub][2])

That is not necessarily bad scientifically, but it is bad presentation. If a referee follows the repo link, they may think the manuscript is stale or strategically withholding results. Before submission, either:

* update Paper 2 to mention the two-ideal results as a subsequent unpublished extension,
* move `newwork/` out of the public repo used for the submitted paper,
* or split the repo into release branches/tags corresponding exactly to each manuscript.

**5. The code path names in the PDF and repo appear inconsistent.**
The manuscript text refers to paths like `direction A/run direction A.py`, but the GitHub repo exposes `direction_A/run_direction_A.py`. The raw script itself says it is `run_direction_A.py` and uses fixed seed 20260609. ([GitHub][3]) That kind of mismatch is minor but embarrassing in a reproducibility-heavy paper.

### Publication verdict for Paper 2

I would recommend **major revision**, not rejection, if submitted to an appropriate niche venue. It is not a high-impact physics result, but it is a credible finite-dimensional algebraic-entanglement no-go analysis. It becomes publishable if you:

* soften “closed,”
* reduce dependence on Paper 1,
* clarify single-copy versus multi-copy invariant data,
* clean the repo/reproducibility story,
* and either remove or integrate the already-computed two-ideal extension.

## Paper 3 review: “Reconstructing the Gauge Coupling from Gauge-Invariant Entanglement Statistics”

### Summary

Paper 3 is the positive benchmark. It studies 2d Yang-Mills on a sphere in the Hartle-Hawking state. The boundary-flux distribution is

[
p_R(t)\propto (\dim R)^2 e^{-(t/2)C_2(R)}, \quad t=g^2A.
]

The manuscript observes that this is a one-parameter exponential family, derives (dS/dt=-(t/4)\mathrm{Var}_t(C_2)), identifies the Fisher information (I(t)=\frac14\mathrm{Var}_t(C_2)), connects entropy susceptibility to reconstructability via (dS/dt=-tI(t)), studies U(1), SU(2), and SU(3), derives a capacity-of-entanglement identity, and shows that multiple intervals increase entropy but not Fisher information because all cuts share one global flux variable. 

### Strengths

This is the **best paper in the set**. It has an exact solvable model, clear formulas, definite theorems, and a narrow, defensible novelty claim.

The manuscript correctly separates **known formulas** from **new framing**. The repo’s `FORMULAS.md` states that the heat-kernel partition function, Hartle-Hawking wavefunction, flux probability distribution, and entropy formula are taken from Donnelly and Gromov-Santos, with convention discrepancies documented. ([GitHub][4]) The paper then adds the estimation-theoretic reading.

The Fisher-information identity is the paper’s most publishable contribution. The claim that entropy susceptibility and statistical reconstructability are the same function in this exactly solvable model is clean and potentially useful. 

The n-interval result is conceptually strong: entropy scales with cut points, but information about (t) does not, because the flux sector is one global random variable. That is exactly the sort of deflationary result that makes the paper more credible.

The paper also appropriately states that this is **reconstruction, not determination**: the inference presupposes the theory, group, state preparation, and one-parameter family; only (g^2A) is recovered; and the edge term is a contested notion of entanglement. 

### Major weaknesses

**1. The novelty is real but modest.**
The main formula is known. The distribution and entropy formula are established in the literature, including 2d nonabelian gauge-theory entanglement calculations by Gromov-Santos and related work on the entropy components in 2d Yang-Mills. ([arXiv][5]) The new part is the Fisher/exponential-family interpretation. That can be publishable, but the manuscript should be a short, sharp note rather than a broad “coupling from entanglement” paper.

**2. “Gauge group of QCD” language should be toned down.**
The paper says SU(3) is the gauge group of QCD and that the coupling is reconstructible in the d=2 world. That is technically scoped, but reviewers may still object. I would replace almost all “QCD group” rhetoric with “SU(3)” and add: “No claim is made about four-dimensional QCD.”

**3. The MLE simulations prove what the exponential-family theorem already implies.**
The Monte Carlo experiments are useful sanity checks, but Cramér-Rao saturation is expected asymptotically for regular one-parameter exponential families. The simulations should move to a compact reproducibility appendix unless there is a finite-sample phenomenon that is genuinely new.

**4. The theorem hypotheses need tightening.**
“Compact connected Lie group” should probably become “nontrivial compact connected Lie group with the heat-kernel Yang-Mills measure in the stated Casimir convention.” The trivial group makes variance zero; groups with torus factors require careful treatment of the representation lattice and Casimir normalization. This is fixable.

**5. Entropy inversion is too idealized.**
The paper acknowledges this, but the abstract still makes exact entropy inversion sound operational. Entropy is not a direct observable; the physically meaningful channel is flux-sector sampling. The title and abstract should emphasize “statistics” more than “entropy.”

**6. The d=4 discussion should either be cut or updated.**
The linked repo now includes a `newwork/u1_4d/REPORT.md` that already reports a conditional phase-by-phase exponential-family picture for compact U(1), including weak-coupling discrete Gaussian sectors, strong-coupling dipole statistics, curved crossover, and a finite topological zero-mode Fisher statement. ([GitHub][6]) If the repo remains public, Paper 3’s future-work language should be synchronized with that report.

### Publication verdict for Paper 3

I would recommend **major revision with a positive outlook**. This is the one I would actually submit first. Good target venues:

* **Journal of Mathematical Physics**
* **Journal of Physics A: Mathematical and Theoretical**
* **Annals of Physics**
* **SciPost Physics Core**
* possibly **JHEP** only if the manuscript is made much sharper and the novelty relative to existing 2d YM entanglement literature is emphasized carefully.

The paper should be shortened, less series-dependent, and framed as:

> “The edge-sector distribution of 2d Yang-Mills is an exponential family; therefore its entropy derivative, Fisher information, and coupling-estimation sensitivity are exactly linked.”

That is a clean publishable statement.

## Cross-paper issues that need fixing before submission

### 1. Too much self-reference

Each paper repeatedly refers to “the series,” “Paper 1,” “Paper 2,” “Paper 3,” and “the companion.” This is fine for arXiv, but journal referees often dislike manuscripts that require reading unpublished companions. Make each paper self-contained.

For Paper 3 especially, reduce the series framing to one paragraph.

### 2. The word “vacuum” should be policed aggressively

Paper 1 already says the three-qubit state is not a QFT vacuum, which is good. But across the series, “vacuum,” “coupling,” “Standard Model,” “QCD group,” and “entanglement” are high-voltage words. Use them only where operationally justified.

### 3. “Machine-verified” is overused

The computations are valuable, but repeated “machine-verified” phrasing can sound defensive. Replace many instances with ordinary scientific phrasing:

* “We verified numerically…”
* “The accompanying code asserts…”
* “The following identities are checked in the repository…”

### 4. The papers should distinguish three objects more sharply

The project moves between:

1. **Subsystem entropy** of qubits.
2. **Algebraic entropy** relative to gauge-invariant algebras.
3. **Flux-sector Shannon/edge entropy** in gauge theory.

The series understands the distinctions, but the rhetoric sometimes blurs them. A skeptical referee will attack this. Add a single notation table early in each paper.

### 5. The edge term’s contested status should remain prominent

Paper 2 and Paper 3 handle this well. Do not weaken it. If anything, make it even more explicit: the positive YM2 result is about a center/edge-sector distribution, not necessarily distillable entanglement.
