# Direction B — Adversarial Literature Verification
## Claim under test: "gauge couplings are entanglement susceptibilities of the vacuum; 1/g_i²(μ) is the edge-mode entanglement coefficient of gauge sector i — and this joins an established literature"

**Method.** 15+ targeted web searches; full texts of the four most load-bearing papers (Kabat hep-th/9503016; Donnelly–Wall 1412.1895 and 1506.05792; Casini–Huerta–Magán–Pontello 1911.00529) were downloaded and grepped for the exact claims, equations, and — critically — for the string "beta function". Abstracts of ~30 further papers were pulled from the arXiv API. All quotes below were extracted verbatim from the papers' texts (modulo LaTeX-to-text artifacts).

## 1. Susskind–Uglum (hep-th/9401070): what is actually proven about G_N renormalization vs entanglement entropy

**The claim found.** Susskind & Uglum, "Black Hole Entropy in Canonical Quantum Gravity and Superstring Theory" (Phys. Rev. D 50 (1994) 2700):

> "The entropy per unit area of a free scalar field propagating in a fixed black hole background is shown to be quadratically divergent near the horizon. It is shown that such quantum corrections to the entropy per unit area are equivalent to the quantum corrections to the gravitational coupling." (abstract)

Concretely: for a **free minimally coupled scalar** at one loop, the divergent entanglement entropy per unit horizon area, S/A ~ 1/ε², is the *same* divergence as the one-loop correction to 1/(4G_N), so the generalized entropy A/4G_N + S_ent is cutoff-independent at this order: δ(1/4G_N)·A + δS_ent = finite. They further *conjectured* (not proved) that in string theory the tree-level Bekenstein–Hawking entropy itself counts open strings with both ends on the horizon, and that the total is finite order by order in string perturbation theory.

**Status: PROVEN (one loop, minimally coupled matter), with a precise later literature.**
- Fursaev–Solodukhin (hep-th/9412020): "One-loop divergences appearing in the entropy of a quantum black hole are **proven** to be completely eliminated by the standard renormalization of both the gravitational constant and other coefficients by the R²-terms" — i.e. the full statement needs the higher-curvature couplings too, not just G_N (first noted by Solodukhin for Schwarzschild).
- Demers–Lafrance–Myers (gr-qc/9503003), Pauli–Villars regulator: "we find that 't Hooft's approach … yields precisely the one-loop renormalization of [Newton's constant]" — explicitly billed as confirming "a suggestion by Susskind and Uglum".
- Larsen–Wilczek (hep-th/9506066): "We consider the suggestion, due to Susskind and Uglum, that these divergences are proportional, and attempt to clarify its precise meaning."
- Modern clean form: **Cooperman–Luty 1302.1878** — "the entanglement entropy is rendered UV-finite by precisely the counterterms required to cancel the UV divergences in the gravitational effective action. In particular, the leading contribution … is given by the renormalized Bekenstein–Hawking formula, in accordance with a proposal of Susskind and Uglum." Subleading divergences are state-dependent and match the Wald entropy on bifurcate Killing horizons.

**Known caveats (all real, all documented):**
1. **Species problem.** S_ent scales with the number of fields; A/4G_N does not visibly. Resolution only in *induced gravity* (Sakharov), where 1/G_N is entirely generated by matter loops — Jacobson gr-qc/9404039; Solodukhin's Living Review 1104.3712 §8 ("species puzzle"). If there is a bare 1/G_N, the identity S_BH = S_ent fails; only the *renormalization* statement survives.
2. **Nonminimal coupling.** For ξRφ² matter, the conical/Wald entropy and the entanglement entropy differ by ξ-dependent horizon terms (Barvinsky–Solodukhin gr-qc/9512047; Solodukhin's review). What matches G_N renormalization is the conical (replica/Wald) entropy, not the naively statistical one.
3. **Gauge fields.** Kabat's negative contact term (item 2). The S-U matching for spin 1 works *only if* the contact term is included in the entropy. Kabat (hep-th/9503016) records that "Susskind and Uglum have suggested that this contact term is responsible for the non-renormalization of Newton's constant in certain supersymmetric theories."
4. **Gravitons.** Worst case: non-smooth heat-kernel behavior at vanishing deficit angle (Fursaev–Miele), non-analytic terms; Solodukhin 1502.03758 attributes the puzzle to orbifold vs n-fold-cover inequivalence; Benedetti–Casini 1908.01800 get log coefficient −61/45 for linearized gravitons in a sphere via mutual information, with no settled anomaly-matching statement. **Open.**

**Verdict on the collaborator's first sentence:** essentially accurate as a one-loop statement for minimal matter, with the qualifier that S-U *suggested* and later authors *proved* it (and that higher-curvature couplings are needed beyond the leading area divergence). It is the entanglement interpretation of the renormalization of the *gravitational* couplings.

## 2. Kabat (hep-th/9503016): the contact term — exact form, sign, and the interpretation debate

**Exact result.** Kabat computes the one-loop conical free energy for spin 0, 1/2, 1. For an abelian gauge field (with ghosts) on the cone of period β times a transverse plane of area A_⊥, in d dimensions (verbatim from the paper, eq. for βF):

> βF_vector = (d−2) βF_scalar + A_⊥ (2π−β) ∫_{ε²}^∞ ds / (4πs)^{d/2}

So the **contact term in the free energy** is ΔβF = A_⊥(2π−β)∫_{ε²}^∞ ds/(4πs)^{d/2}; it vanishes at β = 2π (no conical singularity) and is linear in the deficit angle. Applying S = (β∂_β − 1)βF|_{β=2π}, the entropy organizes through the heat-kernel coefficient c₁ (verbatim):

> c₁ = 1/6 (minimally coupled scalar); 2^[d/2]/12 (Dirac); **(d−2)/6 − 1 (abelian gauge field including ghosts)**
> S = 2π c₁ A_⊥ ∫_{ε²}^∞ ds/(4πs)^{d/2}

The "−1" in c₁ is the contact term. Its entropy contribution is **S_contact = −2π A_⊥ ∫_{ε²}^∞ ds/(4πs)^{d/2} = −A_⊥/(8πε²) in d = 4** — negative and divergent. In d = 4 the total spin-1 coefficient is c₁ = 1/3 − 1 = −2/3 < 0, so (Kabat, intro): "it makes the one loop correction to the black hole entropy **negative**." Kabat is explicit about the conceptual split:

> "At spin one, the black hole entropy is equal to the entropy of entanglement plus a contact interaction with the horizon. The contact interaction cannot be interpreted as entropy of entanglement in quantum field theory."

and gives the geometric picture: "a path integral over particle paths which begin and end on the horizon; it is the field theory limit of the interaction proposed by Susskind and Uglum which couples a closed string to an open string stranded on the horizon."

**Note the direction of Kabat's own statement** (important for the collaborator's paraphrase): the contact term appears **in the black-hole/conical entropy and *not* in the entanglement entropy**. Saying Kabat found a contact term "in gauge-field entanglement entropy" inverts his point; the 20-year puzzle was precisely whether the conical answer *could* be entanglement.

**The interpretation debate (1995–2024), in sequence:**
- Iellici–Moretti hep-th/9703088 (zeta-function status of Kabat's surface term); Cognola et al.; ambiguity discussions (Iellici–Moreira hep-th/9807184).
- Solodukhin 1209.2677: gauge-fixing-parameter independence of the Maxwell effective action and entropy given matched regulators (the term is not a gauge artifact).
- **Donnelly–Wall 1206.5831** ("Do gauge fields really contribute negatively to black hole entropy?"): argued the contact term reflects "a gauge-dependent ambiguity in Wald's formula", is "sensitive to the treatment of infrared modes", and that in 2d reduced phase space quantization "the gauge field contribution to the entropy is positive, finite, and equal to the entanglement entropy." (They partially reversed this assessment in 2014–15: the term is real, and is edge-mode entropy.)
- **Donnelly–Wall 1412.1895 / 1506.05792**: statistical interpretation via edge modes (item 3).
- Soni–Trivedi 1608.00353, Moitra–Soni–Trivedi 1811.06986: the classical (edge) piece is **not distillable** and "does not contribute to the relative entropy or the mutual information, in the continuum limit."
- Witten 1810.11912 ("Open Strings On The Rindler Horizon"): modern string-theoretic attempt at the Susskind–Uglum open-string picture underlying the contact term.
- **Casini–Huerta–Magán–Pontello 1911.00529**: contests the attribution (item 5).
- Ball, Law et al. (JHEP 09 (2024) 032 "Dynamical edge modes and entanglement in Maxwell theory") and Blommaert–Mertens–Verschelde 1801.09910, 1804.07585: ongoing refinements of the edge-sector quantization reproducing Kabat's term.

**Status: the formula is PROVEN; its statistical interpretation was CONTESTED for 20 years and the now-standard edge-mode interpretation is still contested in part (see item 5).**

## 3. Donnelly–Wall (1206.5831, 1406.7304, 1412.1895, 1506.05792): what the edge-mode program actually shows

**1412.1895 (PRL 114, 111603).** Claim (abstract): "this term is the entanglement entropy of edge modes: classical solutions determined by the electric field normal to the entangling surface. We explain how the heat kernel regularization applied to this term leads to the negative divergent expression found by Kabat."

Mechanism: Gauss's law forces E_⊥ to match across the surface, so the reduced density matrix is block-diagonal in E_⊥ and (their eq. (3))

> S = ∫ 𝒟E_⊥ p(E_⊥) [ −ln p(E_⊥) + S(ρ_{E_⊥}) ]

The classical (Shannon) piece −⟨ln p(E_⊥)⟩ is the edge-mode entropy; computed via a brick wall with arbitrary fluxes through it, it reproduces the contact term as ε → 0. The negative sign of the heat-kernel evaluation is explained as an artifact of how that regulator weights the edge partition function — the underlying state-counting quantity is positive.

**1506.05792 (PRD 94, 104053).** Via Kaluza–Klein reduction and 2d duality: "we express the geometric entropy of the electromagnetic field as the entropy of a tower of scalar fields, constant electric and magnetic fluxes, and a contact term … The complete contact term takes the form of **one negative scalar degree of freedom confined to the entangling surface**." And: "the geometric entropy agrees with a statistical definition of entanglement entropy that includes edge modes … This resolves a longstanding puzzle." Note the flux-sector entropy (constant electric/magnetic fluxes on compact manifolds) **does depend on the gauge coupling** — this is the one place in the d=4 story where g² genuinely enters an entropy term, and it is a finite, topological (zero-mode) piece, not the divergent area or log term.

**1406.7304 (Donnelly, CQG 31, 214003)**: nonabelian lattice definition; edge degrees of freedom "transform like surface charges"; tested in 2d Yang–Mills, where the entropy = Shannon entropy of the boundary representation distribution + Σ p_R ln dim R terms, and the representation weights depend on g²A. So in d=2 the edge entropy is an explicit function of the coupling.

**CRITICAL QUESTION — is there a precise statement that the gauge-field EE divergence/log coefficient MATCHES the one-loop beta function of the gauge coupling?**

**NO.** The string "beta function" occurs **zero times** in the full text of both 1412.1895 and 1506.05792 (verified by grep on the ar5iv HTML-extracted text). The coefficients Donnelly–Wall match are:

1. **The conformal anomaly (a-coefficient), not the gauge beta function.** Their exact statements (1412.1895, eqs. (12)–(14)):
   > S_anom ∼ −(31/45) ln(r)  [trace-anomaly prediction for a sphere]
   > S_therm ∼ −(16/45) ln(r)  [Dowker's thermal/bulk calculation, arXiv:1009.3854]
   > S_edge ∼ ln Z ∼ −(1/3) ln(r)  [edge modes: det′(Δ₀)^{+1/2}, "a negative scalar on the entangling surface"]
   −16/45 − 15/45 = −31/45: "when the entropy of the edge modes is added to that of the local degrees of freedom (13), we find the expected agreement with the conformal anomaly (12)."
2. **The renormalization of the gravitational action, not of 1/g².** Verbatim (1412.1895): "If one includes the contact term, the divergences in the Euclidean entropy due to gauge fields are related to **the renormalization of the gravitational action** [Larsen–Wilczek; Cooperman–Luty] in the manner expected from consistency of black hole thermodynamics [Susskind–Uglum; Jacobson; …]."

The structural reason the collaborator's reading is a conflation: Kabat's contact coefficient is part of the heat-kernel coefficient c₁ = (d−2)/6 − 1, which controls the gauge field's contribution to **δ(1/G_N)** (a "gravitational beta function" if one insists), and in the conformal case the log coefficient is the **Euler anomaly a**. Neither is the coefficient b₀ of the running of 1/g_i². In free Maxwell theory in noncompact d=4 the coupling can be absorbed by field redefinition, so the vacuum EE **cannot depend on g at all** — there is nothing for a "1/g² coefficient" to be.

## 4. The algebraic/center approach (Casini–Huerta–Rosabal 1312.1183), Ghosh–Soni–Trivedi, lattice; how center choice interacts with the contact term

**CHR 1312.1183 (PRD 89, 085012).** In gauge theories the gauge-invariant algebra of a region has a **center** (e.g. Wilson loops near the boundary / boundary electric fluxes); entropy is defined for an algebra-with-center via ρ = ⊕_k p_k ρ_k on H = ⊕_k H_A^k ⊗ H_B^k:

> S = H({p_k}) + Σ_k p_k S(ρ_k)   [classical Shannon term + quantum term]

Key findings (abstract, verbatim fragments): "ambiguities arise on the correspondence between algebras and regions"; "it is always possible to choose (in many different ways) local algebras with trivial center, and hence a genuine entanglement entropy"; "in the continuum limit however, ambiguities in the entropy are given by terms local on the boundary … in such a way **relative entropy and mutual information are finite, universal, and gauge independent**."

**Interaction with the contact term.** The **electric center** choice (boundary normal electric fields in the center) makes the classical Shannon term exactly the discrete version of Donnelly–Wall's edge-mode entropy: p_k ↔ p(E_⊥). Ghosh–Soni–Trivedi 1501.02593's extended-Hilbert-space definition "agrees with a particular case of the definition given by Casini, Huerta and Rosabal" (the electric center) for Z_N and U(1), and they argue it reproduces the replica-trick (conical) answer — i.e., **electric center / extended Hilbert space ⇒ Kabat's contact term included**. A trivial-center (gauge-fixed-tree) choice drops the classical term — the contact term is then absent from the entropy, at the cost of a different boundary-local assignment. Casini et al.'s position is that none of these choices is preferred and only MI/relative entropy is physical; Moitra–Soni–Trivedi 1811.06986 prove the classical term "does not contribute to the relative entropy or the mutual information in the continuum limit" (it is dominated by cutoff-scale modes normal to the surface). GST also note the extended-Hilbert-space entropy "does not agree with some standard ways to measure entanglement, like the number of Bell pairs … by entanglement distillation" — the edge term is **non-distillable**.

**Lattice.** Buividovich–Polikarpov 0806.3376: physical Hilbert space doesn't factorize; extension by electric-string endpoints on the boundary required; in 3d Z₂ gauge theory "the entanglement entropy appears to be saturated almost completely by the entropy of string endpoints" — numerical confirmation that the edge/classical term dominates. Donnelly 1109.0036: general three-term decomposition (Shannon over boundary representations + Σ p_R ln dim R + nonlocal quantum term). Itou–Nagata–Nakagawa–Nakamura–Zakharov 1512.01334: first 4d SU(3) Monte Carlo entropic c-function (agrees with free gluons at short distance to ~20%, → 0 beyond ~0.9 fm). Radičević 1509.08478 (weak coupling, any G): in 2+1d the universal EE term is **(1/2) dim(G) log(e²r)** — the gauge coupling enters the universal term, via the softest (edge-adjacent) mode.

**Status: established and (about the ambiguity) essentially settled; the "which choice is right" question is the live boundary of the field.**

## 5. The Maxwell log-coefficient story: Dowker vs Kabat/anomaly, and whether edge modes restore the trace anomaly

The cleanest, most quantitative battleground; this is where "precisely anomaly-related coefficients" is established **and contested in attribution**:

- **Anomaly prediction**: S_log = −(31/45) ln(R/δ) for a Maxwell field on a sphere in d=4 (a-coefficient; Solodukhin 0802.3117 / Casini–Huerta 1102.0440 dictionary, S ⊃ (−1)^{(d−2)/2} 4A log R).
- **Dowker 1009.3854**: direct thermal calculation gives −16/45. Discrepancy −1/3.
- **Donnelly–Wall 1412.1895**: the −1/3 is the edge-mode (contact-term) log: S_edge ∼ −(1/3) ln r; sum −31/45. "Resolved."
- **Eling–Oz–Theisen 1308.4964**: alternative resolution via modified ⟨T_μν⟩ on conformally flat spaces. **Huang 1412.2730**: alternative via a BRST/Gibbons–Hawking-like boundary term. (Multiple competing attributions is itself evidence the matter wasn't a theorem.)
- **Casini–Huerta 1512.06182**: operator-algebra computation: free Maxwell EE on the sphere is **exactly** that of two massless scalars with their l=0 modes removed, giving c_log = 2(−1/90 − 1/6) = **−16/45**; "does not match the coefficient −31/45 in the trace anomaly."
- **Casini–Huerta–Magán–Pontello 1911.00529 (PRD 101, 065020)** — the most sophisticated word so far, and a direct rebuttal of the edge-mode *attribution*:
  - The mismatch is physical: "the logarithmic term is different for a free Maxwell field and a Maxwell field interacting with heavy charges. This is possible because of the presence of superselection sectors in the IR theory."
  - Anomaly restored only with **both** electric charges and magnetic monopoles ("the solution requires both the existence of electric charges and magnetic monopoles"; electromagnetic-duality invariant; each contributes via a factor accounted in mutual information).
  - "the correction due to the coupling with charged vacuum fluctuations, that restores the anomaly coefficient, **is independent of the precise UV dynamics**" — i.e. universal, and in particular **independent of the value of the charge/coupling** and of the charge masses.
  - Direct criticism of the center/edge classical term: "In general local algebras in the continuum theory do not contain a center … Such operator would be too singular … That is why they do not affect the mutual information … the results of [Donnelly–Wall; Soni–Trivedi] highlight that **the ambiguities in the entropy also reach the logarithmic term for some regularizations**." And a sharp technical jab: for the *free* field the total flux vanishes, so the l=0 mode must be dropped from the edge determinant, "giv[ing] a correction to the entropy **+2/3 log(R/ε) instead of −1/3 log(R/ε)**, and the result does not match the anomaly."
  - But also a concession: "In a certain sense, our paper gives a justification for the technical result of these previous calculations" — the arithmetic (a surface scalar determinant supplying −1/3) survives; the *mechanism* is reattributed to charged vacuum fluctuations straddling the surface (the same physics as vacuum polarization/Debye-like screening), not to free-field edge modes.

**Bottom line for item 5:** Yes, there is an established, precise coefficient story — but it is an **anomaly (a-coefficient) story**. The edge-mode contribution restores agreement with the trace anomaly *within the conical/extended-Hilbert-space framework* (Donnelly–Wall, Soni–Trivedi); the algebraic school maintains the free-field answer is −16/45 and the anomaly is restored only by coupling to charged matter + monopoles. Both camps agree on every number; they disagree about which quantity deserves the name "entanglement entropy of the Maxwell field."

## 6. Is there a Susskind–Uglum-type statement for GAUGE couplings ("δ(1/g²) = entanglement of charged fields + edge modes")?

**Not found.** Searches performed (June 2026), among others: `"entanglement entropy" "running of the gauge coupling"`, `"renormalization of the gauge coupling" entanglement edge modes`, `"contact term" "beta function" entanglement gauge`, `"charge renormalization" entanglement horizon Rindler`, `"entanglement susceptibility" gauge coupling`, `"gauge couplings" "from entanglement"`, plus full-text greps of the Donnelly–Wall corpus. No paper states or proves "the renormalization/running of 1/g²(μ) is the entanglement entropy of charged fields plus edge modes across the entangling surface."

What exists instead (the nearest items, in decreasing order of proximity):
1. **Cooperman–Luty 1302.1878**: EE divergences = counterterms of the **gravitational** effective action (G_N, R² couplings). No gauge-background generalization is published as an entropy statement.
2. **CHMP 1911.00529**: charged vacuum fluctuations (the same loop physics as vacuum polarization, i.e., what runs 1/e²) measurably shift the Maxwell EE log coefficient by −1/3. This is a *bona fide* entanglement fingerprint of charge screening — but the shift is **universal and independent of the coupling's value**, which cuts *against* "1/g² is an entanglement coefficient": in the one sharp d=4 computation where charged matter enters an EE coefficient, the coefficient forgets g².
3. **Low dimensions, where coupling genuinely enters edge entropy:** d=2 YM (Donnelly 1406.7304; Blommaert–Mertens–Verschelde 1804.07585): edge sector = particle-on-a-group; p(R) ∝ e^{−g²A C₂(R)}, entropy an explicit function of g²A. d=3 (Radičević 1509.08478): universal term (1/2)dim(G) log(e²r). d=4 compact U(1) (Donnelly–Wall 1506.05792): flux-sector (zero-mode) entropy depends on g². In all cases g² appears **inside** an entropy formula; in no case is 1/g² itself *defined* or *recovered* as an entanglement coefficient.
4. **Trace-anomaly folklore:** in an interacting gauge theory T^μ_μ ⊃ (β(g)/2g³)F², so EE log terms evaluated in F ≠ 0 backgrounds would carry beta-function coefficients via the anomaly. We found no paper that develops this into an edge-mode or Susskind–Uglum-type statement for 1/g².
5. **String-theoretic umbrella:** at sphere level both 1/G_N and 1/g² scale as 1/g_s², so the Susskind–Uglum open-string picture morally covers gauge couplings; Witten 1810.11912 (open strings on the Rindler horizon) is the serious modern attempt at the open-string sector, with no 1/g² statement.
6. **Corner/edge-symmetry program** (Donnelly–Freidel 1601.04744; Geiller–Jai-akson 1912.06025; Freidel–Geiller–Pranzetti 2006.12527 series): establishes *which* boundary degrees of freedom exist for YM and gravity and their symmetry algebras; contains no coupling-renormalization claims.

**Obstruction worth stating plainly:** in noncompact free Maxwell in d=4, g is removable by field redefinition, so vacuum EE is g-independent; any "1/g² from entanglement" statement must lean on compactness (flux quantization) and/or charged matter. The literature contains the ingredients but not the statement.

# Verdict on the proposed reformulation

Clause-by-clause classification of the collaborator's statement:

**(a) Accurate as stated**
- "Susskind–Uglum showed that the renormalization of Newton's constant is [matched by] the entanglement entropy of fields across the horizon" — yes, as a one-loop suggestion made precise and proven by Fursaev–Solodukhin, Demers–Lafrance–Myers, Larsen–Wilczek, Cooperman–Luty (with the R²-coupling refinement and the species/nonminimal/gauge/graviton caveats).
- "Kabat found the 'contact term'" — yes; exact form βΔF = A_⊥(2π−β)∫ds/(4πs)^{d/2}, entropy −A_⊥/(8πε²) in d=4, negative under heat-kernel regularization.

**(b) Accurate with caveats**
- "the Donnelly–Wall edge-mode program [gave the contact term a statistical interpretation]" — yes for the conical/geometric entropy in their brick-wall + flux-sector regularization; **but** the interpretation is contested by Casini–Huerta–Magán–Pontello: the classical center/edge term is regularization- and normalization-dependent, drops out of mutual information, has a free-field zero-mode subtlety (+2/3 vs −1/3), and the physical restoration of the anomaly coefficient arguably requires dynamical electric *and* magnetic charges rather than free-field edge modes.
- Strictly, Kabat's term lives in the *conical/black-hole* entropy and not in the (naive) entanglement entropy — the collaborator's phrase "contact term in gauge-field entanglement entropy" inverts Kabat's own statement, though post-Donnelly–Wall usage is loose on this.

**(c) Wrong or not in the literature**
- "gauge fields' entanglement across a surface carries **precisely beta-function-related** coefficients" — **not in the literature**. The established coefficients are (i) the heat-kernel c₁ controlling δ(1/G_N) and (ii) the Euler anomaly a (−31/45 vs −16/45 vs −1/3 story). "Beta function" appears nowhere in Donnelly–Wall. The phrase is defensible only if "beta function" is stretched to mean "renormalization of the gravitational couplings" or "trace anomaly", which is not the gauge beta function b₀.
- "the running of 1/g² has an entanglement interpretation through the edge modes" — **no published statement**. Worse, the closest established d=4 result (CHMP) shows the charged-matter correction to the EE coefficient is *universal*, i.e. independent of the value of g², and the d=4 free-field EE cannot depend on g at all (field redefinition). The claim is true in spirit only in d=2 (YM₂ edge entropy is a function of g²A) and partially in d=3 (log(e²r)).
- "Therefore the reformulation … is joining an established literature rather than inventing a metaphor" — **overstated**. The edge-mode and renormalization literatures are real and load-bearing, but the specific identification "1/g_i²(μ) = edge-mode entanglement coefficient" does not exist in them, and its application to the three SM factors (values, hierarchy, "why three sectors") appears in no paper we could find (June 2026).

# The defensible conjecture

The strongest version of "couplings as edge-mode entanglement susceptibilities" the literature actually supports:

> **Conjecture (defensible core).** For a compact gauge group factor G_i, the entanglement entropy across a surface Σ, in the extended-Hilbert-space/electric-center definition, contains a classical term S_edge = H[p(E_⊥)]: the Shannon entropy of the distribution of normal electric flux on Σ. (Established: Donnelly 1109.0036; CHR 1312.1183; GST 1501.02593; DW 1412.1895/1506.05792.) The covariance of that distribution is the boundary two-point function ⟨E_⊥(x)E_⊥(y)⟩ — a *flux-fluctuation susceptibility of the vacuum*. In d=2 this term is an explicit, exactly known function of g²A (YM₂); in d=3 its universal part is (1/2)dim(G) log(g²r); in d=4 compact U(1), g² enters through flux-sector weights (a finite zero-mode term), and the coupling to charged matter and monopoles shifts the universal log coefficient by a computable, regularization-independent amount (−1/3, CHMP). **Conjectured extension (not in the literature):** in an interacting d=4 theory, the scale-dependence of the flux distribution p(E_⊥) coarse-grained at scale μ tracks g_i²(μ), so that the running coupling can be *reconstructed from* (not identified with) the edge sector of vacuum entanglement; in a fully induced-coupling scenario (Zee/Terazawa-type, the gauge analog of Sakharov/Jacobson induced gravity), 1/g_i² would be entirely expressible in terms of charged-field entanglement across Σ.

Genuinely open questions attached to this conjecture (none answered in the literature): (1) a d=4 theorem connecting any divergent or universal EE coefficient to b₀ or to the value of 1/g_i² — currently the only sharp d=4 charged-matter effect (CHMP's −1/3) is *coupling-independent*, which is direct evidence that naive versions are false; (2) what fixes the boundary condition / center choice, on which the edge term depends; (3) whether the non-distillability of the edge term (Moitra–Soni–Trivedi) disqualifies it as a "physical" susceptibility; (4) the nonabelian d=4 case, where only lattice data (Itou et al.) and the 2d exact solution exist; (5) anything about *three* sectors or their relative magnitudes — no literature contact at all.

# Relation to our toy (Direction A)

The center/classical term in the algebraic (Casini–Huerta–Rosabal) definition is the exact discrete analog of Donnelly–Wall's edge modes, and this is the bridge to the Direction-A computation. For a finite-dimensional gauge-invariant algebra 𝒜_A with center Z generated by boundary operators (lattice: boundary electric fluxes; toy: the commutant-determined central projectors in the BKOV/commutant construction on 3 qubits), the Hilbert space decomposes as H = ⊕_k H_A^k ⊗ H_B^k and every state gives S(𝒜_A) = H({p_k}) + Σ_k p_k S(ρ_k): a classical Shannon piece over central sectors plus a quantum piece. Donnelly–Wall's S = ∫𝒟E_⊥ p(E_⊥)[−ln p(E_⊥) + S(ρ_{E_⊥})] is literally this formula in the continuum with k → E_⊥, and Kabat's contact term is the heat-kernel evaluation of the H({p_k}) piece. So in the 3-qubit toy, the "edge modes" are nothing but the central sector labels of the chosen subalgebra, the "edge-mode entanglement coefficient" is the sector-distribution entropy H({p_k}), and the toy's tunable parameter that reshapes p_k across sectors plays the role of the coupling — with the CHR caveat imported wholesale: H({p_k}) depends on the choice of subalgebra/center (the discrete analog of the regularization ambiguity), while relative entropies and mutual informations between commuting subalgebras are the choice-independent observables the toy should report.
